## How to read this map

Common statuses include:

- **Working research area** — active subject of investigation; non-canonical.
- **Working concept** — developing conceptual object; non-canonical.
- **Working research distinction** — an active distinction whose terminology may still be refined; non-canonical.
- **Working inquiry** — unresolved question or exploratory direction.
- **Working note** — written working material.
- **Review aid** — material supporting evaluation.
- **External signal** — outside input recorded for comparison.
- **Operational control material** — material governing project work.
- **Canonicalization candidate** — eligible for future canon review but not canon.
- **Historical working area** — part of RABA development history whose current role may have changed.
- **Supporting track** — relevant work that is not currently a central public research direction.
- **Deferred research track** — preserved research direction that is not currently active or public-facing.
- **Blocked / unresolved status** — requires further evidence or Human Owner decision.
- **Canon** — explicitly approved and explicitly marked as canon by the Human Owner.

## High-attention boundaries

### Canonicalization

Canonicalization candidates are not canon.

No concept should become canon through repetition, repository presence, commit history, merge history, public use, external agreement or AI recommendation.

### External signals

External expertise may challenge or strengthen RABA research.

It does not by itself establish:

- validation;
- adoption;
- endorsement;
- partnership;
- compliance;
- commercial readiness.

### Research synthesis

Patterns that recur across cases may be collected and compared internally.

Repeated occurrence does not automatically establish universality or canon.

### Public-facing material

Public clarity must not create the impression that RABA is:

- a finished standard;
- a certified methodology;
- a compliance solution;
- a validated industrial framework;
- an adoption-ready product;
- an established commercial service.

## Open questions for contributors

Examples of useful current questions include:

- Where does autonomous adaptation cease to be execution and become a new decision?
- What makes a transition materially significant?
- When does an existing governing basis cease to be sufficient?
- Can an organization reconstruct why an AI-supported action was authorised, not merely what the system did?
- How should responsibility survive handoffs between humans, models, tools and organizational systems?
- When is source correction insufficient because downstream reliance has already propagated?
- How should recovery be distinguished from correction?
- How can meaningful human control be preserved without requiring meaningless approval of every technical step?
- What evidence should be required before AI-prepared material can legitimately support consequential decisions?
- What counterexamples would falsify current RABA working distinctions?
